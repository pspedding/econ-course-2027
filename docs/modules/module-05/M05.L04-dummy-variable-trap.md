## Explanation

### The Dummy Variable Trap: What It Is

When a categorical variable has $G$ mutually exclusive and exhaustive groups, we need exactly $G - 1$ dummy variables in the regression. Including all $G$ dummies causes **perfect multicollinearity** — a violation of the OLS rank assumption (MLR.3).

#### Why Perfect Multicollinearity Arises

Suppose we have $G = 4$ seasonal quarters: Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4. Define dummies $D_{Q1}, D_{Q2}, D_{Q3}, D_{Q4}$ where each is 1 in the corresponding quarter, 0 otherwise.

For every observation in the dataset:

$$D_{Q1,i} + D_{Q2,i} + D_{Q3,i} + D_{Q4,i} = 1 \quad \text{for all } i$$

But the intercept column in the design matrix is also a column of ones:

$$\mathbf{1} = D_{Q1} + D_{Q2} + D_{Q3} + D_{Q4}$$

This means the intercept column is an **exact linear combination** of the four dummy columns. The design matrix $\mathbf{X}$ does not have full column rank: $\text{rank}(\mathbf{X}) < k+1$, and therefore $\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X}$ is **singular** (non-invertible).

**OLS requires** $(\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X})^{-1}$ to exist. With perfect multicollinearity, this inverse does not exist, and OLS breaks down entirely. Your software will either drop one variable automatically (Stata) or throw an error.

---

### The Fix

**Drop one dummy** (the base/reference category). With $G - 1$ dummies:

$$\mathbf{1} \neq D_{Q1} + D_{Q2} + D_{Q3}$$

because the Q4 observations contribute 0 to the left-hand side but 1 to $\mathbf{1}$. The perfect linear combination is broken, full rank is restored, and OLS can proceed.

---

### Seasonal Dummies Example

**Model** (annual data disaggregated by quarter):

$$\log(sales_t) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 D_{Q1,t} + \beta_2 D_{Q2,t} + \beta_3 D_{Q3,t} + \beta_4 t + u_t$$

Q4 is the **base quarter**. Interpretations:

- $\hat{\beta}_0$: predicted log-sales in Q4, at time $t = 0$
- $\hat{\beta}_1$: difference in log-sales between Q1 and Q4, holding trend constant ($\approx \hat{\beta}_1 \times 100\%$ seasonal effect)
- $\hat{\beta}_2$: Q2 vs Q4 seasonal effect
- $\hat{\beta}_3$: Q3 vs Q4 seasonal effect

To test whether there are any seasonal effects: F-test $H_0: \beta_1 = \beta_2 = \beta_3 = 0$.

---

### Interaction of Two Dummy Variables

Sometimes we want to interact two binary dummies. Suppose:

- $female \in \{0,1\}$: gender indicator
- $graduate \in \{0,1\}$: indicator for having a graduate degree

We can create an interaction dummy:

$$female\_graduate_i = female_i \times graduate_i$$

**Full model**:

$$wage_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1\, female_i + \beta_2\, graduate_i + \beta_3\, (female_i \times graduate_i) + u_i$$

This creates **four groups** with the following predicted wages:

| Group | female | graduate | Predicted wage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Male, non-graduate (base) | 0 | 0 | $\hat{\beta}_0$ |
| Female, non-graduate | 1 | 0 | $\hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1$ |
| Male, graduate | 0 | 1 | $\hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_2$ |
| Female, graduate | 1 | 1 | $\hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 + \hat{\beta}_2 + \hat{\beta}_3$ |

Here $\hat{\beta}_3$ captures whether the **graduate premium is different for women than for men**. If $\hat{\beta}_3 > 0$, graduate women earn more relative to non-graduate women than the corresponding gap among men.

Note: this is equivalent to using 4 dummies from the 2×2 cross-classification, with one (male non-graduate) as the base.

## Worked Example

**Illustrating the Trap**

Suppose we have $n = 6$ observations and one categorical variable (season: Summer, Winter) with dummies $D_S$ and $D_W$.

| Obs | $D_S$ | $D_W$ | Intercept |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Summer) | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 2 (Summer) | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 3 (Summer) | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| 4 (Winter) | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 5 (Winter) | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 6 (Winter) | 0 | 1 | 1 |

The design matrix with intercept, $D_S$, and $D_W$ has three columns. But column 1 = column 2 + column 3 exactly. So $\mathbf{X}'\mathbf{X}$ is:

$$\begin{pmatrix} 6 & 3 & 3 \\ 3 & 3 & 0 \\ 3 & 0 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$

The determinant of this matrix equals $6(9) - 3(9) - 3(9) = 54 - 27 - 27 = 0$. **Singular**: OLS fails.

**Solution**: Drop $D_W$ (Winter becomes the base):

Design matrix now has intercept and $D_S$ only — full rank, OLS proceeds.

$$\hat{y}_i = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 D_{S,i}$$

$\hat{\beta}_0$ = predicted $y$ in Winter; $\hat{\beta}_1$ = Summer vs Winter difference.

## Common Misconception

> **"It doesn't matter which dummy you drop — they're all equivalent."**

**Correction**: It matters a great deal for **interpretation**, though not for **fit**. Whichever category you drop becomes the base/reference group. Every dummy coefficient is interpreted as a difference from that base. If you drop North, coefficients measure South-vs-North, East-vs-North, West-vs-North. If you drop West, you get South-vs-West, East-vs-West, North-vs-West. The $t$-statistics on individual dummies change with the base choice. However, the **fitted values**, **residuals**, **$R^2$**, **SSE**, and **F-tests for joint significance** are all identical regardless of which category you omit. Choose the base category that produces the most interpretable comparisons for your research question.

## Practice Prompts

**Prompt 1 — Identifying the trap**
A researcher has monthly data and creates 12 monthly dummy variables ($Jan, Feb, \ldots, Dec$) then estimates:

$$y_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 Jan_t + \beta_2 Feb_t + \cdots + \beta_{12} Dec_t + \gamma_1 x_t + u_t$$

(a) Why will OLS fail here?
(b) What is the fix?
(c) If the researcher runs this in Stata, what will Stata do automatically?

---

**Prompt 2 — Seasonal dummies**
You have quarterly GDP growth data from 2000 Q1 to 2022 Q4. You include Q1, Q2, Q3 dummies (Q4 is base) plus a linear time trend.

(a) Interpret the coefficient on Q1.
(b) To test whether Q1 and Q2 have the same seasonal effect, what test would you use? Write the null hypothesis formally.
(c) To test whether there are any seasonal effects at all, write the null hypothesis and describe the test statistic.

---

**Prompt 3 — Dummy interaction**
Consider the interaction model:

$$wage_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1\, union_i + \beta_2\, black_i + \beta_3\, (union_i \times black_i) + u_i$$

(a) Write out the predicted wage for each of the four groups (non-union/white; union/white; non-union/black; union/black), treating "white non-union" as the base.
(b) Interpret $\hat{\beta}_3 = -1.2$.
(c) A researcher omits $black_i$ from the model but includes $union_i \times black_i$. Why is this a mistake?
